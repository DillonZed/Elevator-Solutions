Elevator consulting explained

What Does an Elevator Consultant Do?

An elevator consultant works for the building owner. Before you sign a maintenance contract or approve a modernization, you have someone on your side who knows the industry as well as the companies you're dealing with.

The short answer

We level the playing field

Most building managers deal with elevators a few times a year. The companies that sell and service them deal with elevators every day. That knowledge gap shows up in contracts, quotes and repair bills.

An independent consultant closes the gap. We assess the equipment, review what you're paying for, write the specifications, run the tender and check the work, all on the owner's behalf.

Five reasons

Why buildings hire an elevator consultant

  1. An independent assessment

    A consultant looks at the elevators with the owner's interests in mind. Our job is to find the problems, and our view is different from that of the company servicing the equipment.

  2. Protect your investment

    Elevators are one of a building's largest investments and central to how it runs. We tell you how well your service provider is maintaining that investment.

  3. Better reliability, less liability

    Better maintenance means more reliable elevators, fewer tenant complaints and lower liability exposure.

  4. Better service for the money

    Long shutdowns and expensive repairs are common. Regular consulting involvement usually improves the maintenance being done, which extends equipment life and delays large capital costs.

  5. Better planning and budgeting

    We can confirm whether it is time to plan a modernization or just time for a new service provider, with a fair estimate of the equipment's remaining life.
